Research framework

Making the scientific objects of cognition computable

My work starts from a simple bottleneck: cognitive science often stores its core objects as prose, paradigm names, local datasets, post-hoc model fits, and contested construct labels. I build methods that turn those objects into things that can be measured, compared, verified, reused, and perturbed.

This is newly possible because AI tools let us process scientific material at a scale that used to be inaccessible: thousands of papers, hundreds of behavioral datasets, and many task variants. The challenge is to make that scale scientifically meaningful by representing constructs, evidence, tasks, and mechanisms in forms that can be compared.

Data foundation

Reusable behavioral evidence

I develop ways to turn static, incomplete behavioral datasets into living evidence bases. Connecting related datasets makes it possible to study patterns that remain invisible within any single archive while preserving where the evidence came from.

Theory foundation

Comparable tasks and mechanisms

I represent task relations through behavior and computation, asking which patterns and mechanisms generalize across tasks and which remain specific to a particular setting.

One framework, different scientific objects

Across these directions, I make objects that cognitive science often leaves implicit available for measurement and comparison.

01

Hypotheses and literature

Construct-level matching turns "has this been tested?" into a computable comparison between claims and evidence, abstracting both hypotheses and papers to the constructs they contain.

02

Behavioral datasets

Connecting scattered behavioral datasets into a shared evidence base makes it possible to reuse and combine data across studies, revealing patterns that no single dataset can show.

03

Task relations

Cross-task transfer measures whether one task exports behavioral patterns or mechanisms that another task receives.

04

Paradigm structure

Label-free event-state graphs make cognitive-control paradigms comparable by structure rather than by name.

05

Model mechanisms

SAE and ablation analyses ask whether LLM internals represent task constructs or mechanisms that correspond to human strategies and cognitive theory.

06

Subjective task representations

Language and behavioral trajectories help recover how people represent a decision problem, and whether laboratory measures capture the mechanisms used in consequential choices.
