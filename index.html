Lan Pan

Computational cognitive science

How can existing research lead to new knowledge about the mind? This question guides my work toward a computable, cumulative cognitive science. I develop ways to represent and connect tasks, constructs, hypotheses, literature evidence, datasets, behavioral trajectories, and latent decision states. These scientific objects are often defined through natural language, paradigm names, or isolated studies; my aim is to make them computable, comparable, testable, and reusable.

The purpose is to ask when findings support the same explanation, which cognitive processes are shared or context-dependent, and where theories hold, fail, or need revision. Clearer representations and measurements should help us predict behavior we have not yet observed and decide how to test, overturn, or improve our explanations. Computational representations, analysis agents, and large-scale data are means toward this scientific goal.

I work with Robert C. Wilson and Hanbo Xie at Georgia Tech, Sebastian Musslick at Osnabrück University, and Akshay K. Jagadish and Younes Strittmatter at Princeton University.
