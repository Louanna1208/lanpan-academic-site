Research framework

From isolated findings to cumulative cognitive science

Cognitive science can produce reliable findings without making them easy to combine. Hypotheses are expressed in prose, tasks are grouped by inherited paradigm names, behavior is stored in local datasets, and mechanisms are inferred through study-specific models. Studies that use the same label may not measure the same thing, while studies with different labels may share an underlying structure.

Modern AI changes what is practical. I use language models, representation learning, behavioral modeling, and analysis systems as measurement tools to align concepts and evidence at scale. The aim is not simply to process more material, but to make relations among studies testable: what evidence can be combined, which patterns transfer, which mechanisms are shared, and where generalization breaks down.

Data foundation

Evidence across studies

A large cross-task collection of behavioral datasets provides a basis for reuse across studies. My work focuses on the agent and analytical tools that let researchers query this collection, identify relevant datasets, and combine them to investigate patterns that no single dataset can reveal.

Theory foundation

Structure across tasks

Task relationships should be inferred from observable structure, behavioral transfer, and model mechanisms rather than inherited labels alone. Converging evidence across these levels can reveal which processes generalize across tasks and which remain specific to a particular setting.

A connected research program

Each direction addresses a requirement for cumulative explanation: align what studies mean, connect what they measure, map how tasks relate, and test whether inferred mechanisms generalize.

01 / ALIGN

Hypotheses and literature

Representing hypotheses and papers at the construct level makes it possible to test whether a claim has already been examined, even when wording and disciplinary labels differ.

02 / CONNECT

Behavioral datasets

Connecting scattered behavioral datasets into a shared evidence base makes it possible to reuse and combine data across studies, revealing patterns that no single dataset can show.

03 / REPRESENT

Paradigm structure

Event-state representations describe what a task requires, making paradigms comparable by structure rather than by name. Cognitive control provides a focused domain for developing and testing this approach.

04 / MAP

Task relations

Cross-task transfer tests whether learned behavioral structure carries from one task to another, revealing shared and task-specific patterns that names or instructions may miss.

05 / TEST

Model mechanisms

When language models perform cognitive tasks, representation analysis and intervention test whether their strategies instantiate mechanisms that correspond to human behavior and cognitive theory.

06 / GENERALIZE

Subjective task representations

Language and behavioral trajectories reveal how people construe a decision problem, allowing us to test whether mechanisms inferred from laboratory measures also explain consequential real-world choices.

Together, these methods support theories that do more than fit one experiment: they organize evidence and explain when behavior and mechanisms generalize across tasks and contexts.
